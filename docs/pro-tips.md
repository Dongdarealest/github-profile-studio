# Pro tips: what separates a profile that gets followed from one that gets closed

Collected from studying profiles with real followings. None of this costs anything.

## 1. One accent colour, no exceptions

The single highest-leverage rule. Every widget ships with its own default palette, so an
uncustomised profile shows six clashing colour schemes at once. Pick one hex and put it in
every URL. Instantly, the page reads as *designed by a person with taste*.

## 2. Hierarchy: a poster, a line, then proof

- **First screen:** your name large, one typing line that says what you do, nothing else competing.
- **Second screen:** proof — pinned work with honest one-line descriptions.
- **Third screen:** numbers — stats, streak, graph. Numbers support the story; they are not the story.

## 3. Air is a feature

Profiles fail by addition. Every extra badge makes the others worth less. If a block does not
answer *who are you, what did you build, how do I reach you*, cut it. The Gallery template in
this kit exists to prove how far restraint goes.

## 4. Write descriptions like a person

"Pinned repo: a REST API" tells a visitor nothing. "Cuts invoice entry from 20 minutes to 2"
gives them a reason to click and a reason to follow. One sentence, one concrete outcome.

## 5. Your name field and bio are search results

GitHub search reads your name, bio and location. Put the words people search for —
your stack, your city, what you build — in plain text there, not only inside images.
Images are invisible to search; the sentence under them is not.

## 6. One call to action

Follow, hire, collaborate, sponsor — pick the one you actually want and make it the only
button-styled element near the top. Three competing CTAs convert like zero.

## 7. Keep it alive without touching it

Dynamic blocks (stats, streak, activity, snake, metrics) make a profile feel inhabited.
A profile whose newest visible date is last year reads as abandoned, even if you shipped yesterday.

## 8. Dark and light both

Half your visitors are in light mode. Check your profile in both. Transparent or
background-matched blocks survive both; hard-coded dark cards on a light page look broken.

## 9. The 32-pixel test

Your avatar and your first banner line must survive being seen tiny and fast — in a comment
thread, in a follower list. Serif poster type and one accent colour pass; busy collages do not.

## 10. Share the making, not just the result

A short post — "I rebuilt my GitHub profile in 60 seconds with this kit, here is before/after" —
outperforms the profile itself for discovery. Before/after screenshots are the format.
That loop is how profile kits spread, and why this one is built to be shared.
